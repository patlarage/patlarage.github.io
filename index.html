🚀 What This App Actually Does

When you use Claude — especially Claude Code — there are invisible numbers running in the background: how many tokens you have sent and received, how much of the context window is currently filled, and how close you are to the usage limits on your Anthropic account. Normally you only find out when something slows down or stops working.

ClaudeUsageMonitor brings those numbers out into the open. It is a miniature monitoring window built specifically for Windows. It stays on top of your other windows, updates itself automatically, and uses very little memory or processing power. You can leave it running all day and forget it is there — until you need it.

It is designed for anyone who relies on Claude during the workday: writers, researchers, students, support teams, and yes, developers using Claude Code. If you have ever wondered “do I still have room left before I hit a limit?”, this app answers that question instantly.

✨ What You Can See at a Glance

  • Token usage — a live running count of the tokens you have used, so you can see how quickly a long session adds up.
  • Context window fill level — a clear visual showing how full your current context window is and how much room is left before it resets.
  • Anthropic account limits — your account's usage limits, shown as progress you can read in a split second instead of guessing.
  • Claude Code activity — a compact readout built for people running Claude Code on their machine.
  • Always-on-top mini window — small enough to tuck into a corner of your screen and keep visible while you work.
  • Automatic refresh — the numbers update on their own, with no buttons to press and no page to reload.
  • Native Windows speed — a lightweight Win32 application, not a heavy browser wrapper, so it starts fast and stays out of the way.

🖥️ Everyday Ways People Use It

Before a long writing session. Open the monitor first and see how much room you have. If your context window is already fairly full, you know it is a good moment to start a fresh conversation rather than fight against a limit halfway through a paragraph.

While running Claude Code. Long automated sessions can burn through tokens quickly. A quick glance at the monitor tells you whether your current run is comfortable or whether it is time to wrap up and split the work into smaller pieces.

When sharing a computer or an account. If other people use the same Anthropic account, the monitor gives you a shared, honest view of how much of the allowance has already been consumed today.

As a gentle early warning. Instead of discovering a limit the hard way — mid-task, mid-thought — you see the numbers climbing in the corner of your screen and can plan around them.

🔒 Privacy and Safety

ClaudeUsageMonitor runs entirely on your own PC. It reads the usage information already present on your machine and displays it in a small window. It does not upload your conversations anywhere, it does not require you to type in a password, and it does not store your account credentials.

The app has no advertising, no telemetry of its own, and no background services. When you close it, it stops. Everything it shows stays on your computer.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Is this an official Anthropic product?
No. ClaudeUsageMonitor is an independent, community-made utility built by a user who wanted a clearer view of their own usage. It is not affiliated with or endorsed by Anthropic.

Do I need to be a programmer to use it?
Not at all. If you can download a file and double-click it, you can use this app. There is no command line, no configuration file, and no setup wizard.

Does it work with Claude in the browser as well as Claude Code?
The monitor is built around the usage information available on your Windows machine, which includes Claude Code. Its purpose is to show you tokens, context window fill, and account limits regardless of which Claude surface you are using.

How often does the information update?
The window refreshes on its own while it is open, so the numbers you see are current. There is no manual refresh button to remember.

Where do I get updates?
New versions are published on the same releases page you used to download the app: github.com/patlarage/ClaudeUsageMonitor/releases. Check back there whenever you want the newest build.

Why does Windows show a warning the first time I run it?
Windows treats small, newly published independent apps cautiously. Clicking More info and then Run anyway lets the app start normally.

🧭 Tips for Getting the Most Out of It

Place the window in the top-right corner of your screen, just below the menu bar. That spot is out of the way of most work but still in your field of view, so the numbers register without you having to look for them.

Glance at it before you start a big task rather than during one. Knowing your starting position makes it easy to judge whether a long conversation is realistic or whether you should break the work into shorter pieces.

Keep an eye on the context window reading in particular. A context that is nearly full can quietly change the quality of your results, and the monitor gives you an early nudge to start fresh.
